01 Getting started

  1. Install on each device you use Medical Casebook runs on Mac, iPhone and iPad. To sync, sign in to the same Apple Account on every device, with iCloud turned on. There is no app account to create.
  2. Capture a topic Open Capture, type the thing you need to look up (or tap the microphone and say it), and press Save or Return. New topics start as Incomplete.
  3. Answer it on the Mac Open the topic from Topics. Add tags and sources, then write the Synthesis Note in sections. Use Add In-Text Citation to cite a source as you write.
  4. Mark what you want to remember Select a phrase and choose Hide for study. When the topic is done, set its status to Answered.
  5. Study Open Study each day. Your hidden phrases — and, with Include images on, your starred images and collections — come back as spaced-repetition flashcards.
  6. Jot and record anywhere Use Sticky Notes for anything that isn't a topic yet, and file it later. Turn on Recording to record a lecture into a note.

02 Frequently asked questions

Sync & data

How does sync work?

Your casebook is stored on each device and synced through Apple's iCloud, in a private database in your own iCloud account. Any Mac, iPhone or iPad signed in to the same Apple Account stays up to date automatically. There's no sign-in inside the app.

Your topics, notes, images, sticky notes, follow ups and study history sync. Some settings are set separately on each device, such as Recording, new cards a day, Include images and your daily goal.

If two devices edit the same sticky note at once, the edits are merged, or a “(conflicting copy)” note is kept so nothing is lost.

My content isn't syncing between devices.
  1. Check that every device is signed in to the same Apple Account.
  2. In your device's iCloud settings, make sure iCloud is on for the app. It's listed under its home-screen name, Casebook.
  3. Check that each device is online. Sync pauses without a connection and catches up when you're back.
  4. Check your iCloud storage. A full iCloud account can stop sync.
  5. Open the app on both devices and give it a few minutes.
  6. Still stuck? Restart both devices, then email us with which devices are involved and what isn't arriving.
Is my content backed up? What happens if I delete the app?

With iCloud on, your casebook is kept in your private iCloud database. Reinstall the app on a device signed in to the same Apple Account and it comes back.

If iCloud is off for the app, your casebook exists only on that device and in any backups you make of it, such as iCloud Backup, a Finder backup or Time Machine. Deleting the app from an iPhone or iPad then deletes it. Keep iCloud on if you want a copy that survives.

The speech model isn't included in backups. If Recording is on, the app downloads it again when it's needed.

I deleted something by accident.

Open Trash (in the sidebar on the Mac; under More on iPhone and iPad). Deleted topics — with everything in them — Sticky Notes and Follow Ups stay there for 30 days. Choose Restore to put one back.

Images don't go to Trash. On iPhone and iPad, deleting an image asks you to confirm first; on the Mac, Delete Image removes it immediately. Either way it can't be undone.

How do I delete my data?
  • Individual items: delete them, then use Delete Now or Empty Trash in Trash. Anything left in Trash is deleted after 30 days.
  • The speech model: on the Mac, open Settings › Recording, turn off Record in Sticky Notes, then choose Remove. On iPhone and iPad, go to More › Recording and choose Remove from This Device.
  • Everything on one device: on iPhone and iPad, delete the app. On the Mac, delete the app, then also delete the folder ~/Library/Containers/com.radiology.Radiology-Casebook, which holds its data and the speech model; moving the app to the Trash leaves that folder behind. Transcript files and PDFs you saved elsewhere are ordinary files to delete in Finder.
  • Your iCloud copy: delete the app's data in your device's iCloud settings, where you manage iCloud storage.

The developer holds no copy of your data, so there's nothing to delete on our side — and we can't restore anything for you. See the privacy policy.

Can I store patient information?

Please don't. Medical Casebook is a personal study tool for what you learn from articles, textbooks and teaching. It isn't a medical record, it isn't designed for HIPAA compliance, and the developer doesn't sign business associate agreements.

Leave out names, dates of birth, record or accession numbers and faces, and remove identifying text from images before you import them. Images are stored as you import them, including any metadata embedded in the file, so remove identifying metadata too.

The same goes for recordings: don't record sessions where patients are identified, and remove any identifiers that end up in a transcript.

Does the app collect any of my data?

No. There are no developer servers, accounts, analytics or tracking. The app goes online only for iCloud sync, source lookups you ask for, the speech-model download, and Apple's speech recognition when your device can't dictate on its own. The privacy policy lists every connection.

Recording

How do I set up recording?

On the Mac: open Settings, choose Recording, and turn on Record in Sticky Notes. The speech model (about 630 MB) downloads. Then open a sticky note and press Record. You can record from the Microphone, or from System Audio for a Zoom or Teams lecture or a video. A chip in the title bar shows the recording from any section. When you stop, the transcript is added to the bottom of the note, or saved to a folder if you've chosen that.

On iPhone and iPad: go to More › Recording and turn on Record in Sticky Notes. Then press Record at the foot of a sticky note. When you stop, the transcript is added to the bottom of the note. If you leave the note while recording, a Recording · Stop bar sits above the tab bar.

Recording makes a verbatim transcript of English speech, on your device. It's automatic, so it can mishear: check important details against the source. The audio itself is never saved.

Which iPhones and iPads can record?

Recording needs a device that can run Whisper large-v3-turbo:

  • iPhone: any iPhone with an A15 chip or newer — iPhone 13 or later, or iPhone SE (3rd generation).
  • iPad: an iPad with an A15, A16, A17 Pro or M-series chip.

Recording isn't supported on older chips (A14 and earlier) — for example iPhone 11 and 12, iPhone SE (2nd generation), iPad Air (4th generation) and iPad (10th generation). There's no smaller fallback model. Everything else in the app works as usual.

On the Mac, a Mac with Apple silicon is recommended for Recording.

How big is the speech model, and when does it download?
  • It's about 630 MB (M1 iPads use a 625 MB compressed build of the same model), downloaded from Hugging Face when you turn Recording on.
  • On iPhone and iPad, Wi-Fi is recommended. If a download is interrupted, it picks up where it left off.
  • On iPhone and iPad, the app checks your free space first and won't start the download unless about 1 GB is free: the download, plus room for the system to prepare the model.
  • The model isn't included in device backups. If Recording is on and the model is missing, for example after restoring a device, the app fetches it again — on iPhone and iPad only over Wi-Fi.
  • To free the space on the Mac, turn off Record in Sticky Notes in Settings › Recording and choose Remove. On iPhone and iPad, choose Remove from This Device in More › Recording.
Where did the AI summary go?

The on-device summarizer was removed in October 2026. Recording now makes a verbatim transcript only, with nothing summarized or rewritten. The app deletes the files the old summarizer left behind.

Can I save transcripts to a folder instead of a note?

On the Mac, yes. In Settings › Recording, turn on Save transcripts to a folder and choose a folder. Each recording is saved as its own .txt file, and the sticky note is left unchanged. On iPhone and iPad the transcript always goes into the note.

Tips for better recordings
  • On iPhone and iPad, recording continues with the phone locked.
  • The phone uses its built-in microphone, even when AirPods are connected, so sit where it can hear the speaker.
  • Many words Whisper was unsure of appear as [word?], and stretches it couldn't make out as [inaudible]. Not every error is marked, so check important details against the source.
  • Silence is skipped, along with common stock phrases (such as “Thank you.”) that Whisper sometimes produces from it.
  • Recording transcribes English speech.
  • Each transcribed block is labeled “Transcribed from a voice recording. May contain errors.”
  • If a recording is interrupted, its words are delivered the next time you open the app.
  • Record only where you're allowed to, and if you record a call or meeting, make sure the other participants agree. Don't record sessions where patients are identified.
Record says access was denied.

Allow the microphone for the app in Settings › Privacy & Security › Microphone (iPhone and iPad) or System Settings › Privacy & Security › Microphone (Mac). To record System Audio on the Mac, also allow it under Screen & System Audio Recording. Only the audio is kept; nothing on your screen is saved.

Sticky Notes & writing

Can I edit Sticky Notes on iPhone and iPad?

Yes. On Mac, iPhone and iPad you can create, edit and search notes, use bold, italic and underline, pick a color (Plain, Sand, Clay, Sage or Slate), record, file and trash them. Edits sync between your devices.

On iPad the list sits beside the note, with keyboard shortcuts: ⌘N new note, ⌥↑/⌥↓ to step through notes, ⌘⇧A and ⌘⇧N to file.

How does filing a sticky note work?
  • Attach to… (⌘⇧A) copies the note onto an existing topic.
  • Make a topic (⌘⇧N) turns it into a new topic.
  • Also file to… files the same note to another topic.

Filing copies the text, with its bold, italic and underline, and marks the note as filed. The note itself stays in Sticky Notes. On the Mac the sidebar shows how many notes are still unfiled, and ⌘⌫ moves a note to Trash.

On iPhone and iPad, if a topic's note hasn't finished syncing, or uses a font the device doesn't have, filing is refused with an explanation. Try again later, or file the note from the Mac.

Can I write topic notes on iPhone or iPad?

The Synthesis Note — sections, formatting, citations and Hide for study — is written on the Mac. On iPhone and iPad it opens as a reader, marked “Read-only · edit on Mac”, that remembers the last section you read. You can still change a topic's status, tags, sources and images there, and quiz yourself with Study Mode.

For quick writing on the go, use Sticky Notes and file them onto the topic later.

How do I add sources and citations?

Add a Journal Article by DOI or PubMed ID, a Website by URL, or a Book by ISBN, and fetch its details. On iPhone and iPad you can scan a book's ISBN barcode with the camera.

While writing on the Mac, choose Add In-Text Citation to place a numbered marker. Numbers follow the order sources first appear in the note and renumber themselves as you edit. The reference list uses AMA style.

Capture won't save my topic.

Capture blocks a name that exactly matches an existing topic or group, ignoring capitals, and shows a warning such as “There is already a topic with that name.” Open the existing topic instead, or reword the new one.

How do I reorder image collections, or use the Repository?

In a topic, drag collections into order on the Mac, or use Move Up and Move Down on any device.

Star an image or a whole collection to add it to the Repository. There you can filter by Specialty, Anatomy, Disease, Kind and Modality, and sort by date, caption, collection or topic. Removing something from the Repository never deletes it; it stays with its topic.

Does the Mac app work in Split View?

Yes. The Mac app opens full screen, and it resizes for Split View and narrow windows; the Topics index packs into fewer columns as the window narrows. On iPad, the app works in all orientations and with iPadOS multitasking, including resizable windows.

PDF export

How do I export a topic as a PDF?

On the Mac, open the topic and choose Share, then Save PDF… or Share PDF…. On iPhone and iPad, export from the topic page and use the share sheet to save or send it.

The PDF is laid out like a journal article: a running header, the title, tags, a two-column body with a drop cap, figures in a framed two-up grid with their annotations drawn on and listed under each caption, and references.

Can a figure appear in the middle of the PDF?

Yes. On the Mac, right-click an image, choose Add to Section and pick a section. The image prints right after that section instead of with the figures at the end, in PDFs exported from any device. Choose Remove from Section to undo it.

What is the Fill in the Blank worksheet?

A PDF where every phrase you marked Hide for study becomes a numbered blank, with an answer key at the end. There is also a version without the answer key, for handing out. On iPhone and iPad, use the Include answer key switch.

Study, Follow Ups & Dashboard

How are flashcards made and scheduled?
  • Phrase cards: each phrase marked Hide for study in an Answered topic becomes a cloze card built from its sentence.
  • Image cards: with Include images on, a starred picture is shown bare; the answer is its title, caption and annotations.
  • Collection cards: a starred collection becomes one card you page through.

Scheduling follows Anki's classic SM-2 rules. Grade each card Again, Hard, Good or Easy (keys 1–4). The study day starts at 4 AM. You can set how many new cards a day to see (20 by default), use Practice anyway without changing due dates, Undo with ⌘Z, and Save and Exit to Resume later.

Why isn't a hidden phrase showing up as a card?
  • Phrase cards come only from topics marked Answered.
  • Cards from the same paragraph are held back as “siblings” so one doesn't give away the next; they come on another day.
  • You may have reached your new-cards-a-day limit.
How do I zoom into an image while studying?

Open the full-size viewer. Pinch or press ⌘+ to zoom, drag to pan, double-click to fit, and use ←/→ to page through a collection.

What's the difference between Study and Study Mode?

Study Mode works inside a topic's note: it covers your hidden phrases so you can reveal them one at a time (on iPhone and iPad, as a queue of cards with Reveal answer and Got it / Missed). Study is the flashcard section, which schedules those phrases and your starred images with spaced repetition.

Will Follow Ups remind me?

No. Follow Ups don't send notifications. Each has a due day and sorts into Overdue, Today, Later and Completed; open Follow Ups to see what's due. On the Mac the sidebar shows a count.

Where is the Dashboard on iPhone and iPad?

Under More. It shows your Streak, Today's Goal, By Specialty, Activity and Flashcards, in a compact layout for phones and narrow iPad windows. Reorder the cards in Dashboard Settings.

03 Requirements

Mac
macOS 26.3 or later.
iPhone & iPad
iOS or iPadOS 26.4 or later.
App version
Version 2.9 or later for everything on this page.
Recording
Transcribes English speech. On iPhone and iPad: an iPhone with an A15 chip or newer (iPhone 13 or later, or iPhone SE 3rd generation), or an iPad with an A15, A16, A17 Pro or M-series chip, and about 1 GB of free space for the model. On the Mac, Apple silicon is recommended.
Sync
Optional. Needs iCloud; no app account.
Network
Only for iCloud sync, source lookups you ask for, the model download, and Apple's speech recognition for Capture dictation when your device can't do it on its own.

04 Report a bug or suggest a feature

Email proceduralist@proceduralist.io. For a bug, please include:

  1. App version. On the Mac it's in Settings › General. If you're not sure on iPhone or iPad, tell us roughly when you last updated.
  2. Device — for example “MacBook Air M2” or “iPhone 15”.
  3. OS version — macOS, iOS or iPadOS.
  4. What you did, what you expected, and what happened instead. Steps to reproduce help most.
  5. For sync problems, which devices are involved. For recording problems, whether you used the Microphone or System Audio.
  6. A screenshot, if you can.

Please don't send screenshots or notes that contain patient information.

Ideas are welcome too: send a feature idea.
