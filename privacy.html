Privacy Policy

Effective October 23, 2026

Medical Casebook is made by Ryan Fredette, an independent developer (“we” or “the developer” in this policy). This policy explains what the app does with your information. You can reach us at proceduralist@proceduralist.io.

The short version

Medical Casebook does not collect your data. The developer runs no servers, and the app has no accounts, no analytics, no advertising and no tracking.

What you create stays on your devices and, if you use iCloud, in your own private iCloud database, which the developer cannot access. Recordings are transcribed on your device, and the audio is never saved.

The app goes online only for iCloud sync, for source lookups you ask for, to download its speech model if you turn on Recording, and — when your device can't recognize speech on its own — to use Apple's speech recognition when you dictate a topic.

01 What we collect

Nothing. The app sends nothing to the developer. There is no developer server, no account or sign-in, and no analytics, crash-reporting or advertising code in the app. We never learn your name, your email address or anything you write in the app.

The app's App Store privacy label is Data Not Collected. Its privacy manifest declares no tracking, no tracking domains and no collected data types.

Like most apps, Medical Casebook writes diagnostic messages to your device's system log, which your operating system manages. The app never sends the log anywhere. On the Mac, when the app repairs duplicate copies of a note left behind by iCloud sync, those messages can include the topic's title and short excerpts of the note's text.

If you email us, we receive what you send — your email address and your message — and use it to reply to you.

02 Where your data lives

On your device

Your casebook is stored on your device using Apple's SwiftData framework. That includes your topics and groups, tags, Synthesis Notes, sources, images and their annotations, collections, Sticky Notes (including any transcripts in them), Follow Ups, your flashcard study history, and Related Lectures links. Like other app data, it's included in the device backups you make, such as iCloud Backup, a Finder backup or Time Machine.

In your private iCloud database

If you're signed in to iCloud, the app syncs that content between your devices through Apple's CloudKit, using the app's private database in your own iCloud account. The app uses no public or shared iCloud databases. The developer cannot access your private database; your data there is governed by Apple's iCloud terms and privacy policy.

The app registers for silent push notifications only so that iCloud can tell it new data is ready to sync. It never shows you notifications.

Other things kept on the device

  • Preferences, such as whether Recording is on, your flashcard settings and, on the Mac, the transcript folder you picked, are stored on that device.
  • The speech model (about 630 MB) is stored in the app's own storage and excluded from device backups.
  • Recording recovery files. While you record, transcribed words — text only, never audio — are written to a small file on the device so they aren't lost if the app is interrupted. The file is deleted once its words reach your note. If one is left over, its words are delivered the next time you open the app, and then it is removed.
  • Transcript files (Mac, optional). If you turn on Save transcripts to a folder, each recording is saved as a .txt file in the folder you choose, instead of being added to the note. Those are ordinary files in a folder you control.
  • Related Lectures (Mac). When you link a lecture deck to a topic, the app stores the file's name, the lecturer, your note and the file's last-known location in your casebook, so they sync like the rest of it. The app never uploads the lecture file itself.
  • Temporary and cache files. To share a PDF or an image, the app writes a temporary file for the share sheet. Source lookups (books, articles and web pages) may be cached on the device, and websites you fetch details from may store cookies in the app.

03 On-device processing

Recording into Sticky Notes

Recording uses Whisper (large-v3-turbo), run on your own device by the open-source WhisperKit package. English speech is transcribed verbatim on the device. The audio is held in memory only and is never written to disk, and neither the audio nor the transcript is sent to the developer or to any third party.

The transcript goes into the sticky note you recorded in, and is stored and synced like the rest of your casebook. On the Mac you can instead save it to a folder, as described above.

There is no language model and no AI summary. An earlier on-device summarizer was removed in October 2026, and the app deletes the files it left behind.

Once the speech model and its tokenizer are on your device, recording works offline and normally makes no network requests. If any of those files are missing or damaged, the app downloads them again from Hugging Face. Your audio and transcripts are never sent.

Recording System Audio (Mac)

On the Mac you can record System Audio, for example a Zoom or Teams lecture or a video. macOS controls this through the Screen & System Audio Recording permission. The app keeps only the audio. macOS requires a screen stream to capture audio, so the app asks for the smallest one possible (2×2 pixels) and discards it without reading it. Nothing on your screen is saved or sent anywhere. If you record a call or meeting, make sure you have the other participants' permission.

Dictating a topic in Capture

The microphone button in Capture uses Apple's speech recognition. Where your device supports on-device recognition, the audio stays on the device. Where it doesn't, your dictation is sent to Apple to be recognized, under Apple's privacy policy. This happens only while you are dictating.

04 Network connections the app makes

All connections are outgoing and encrypted (HTTPS); the app accepts no incoming connections. Like any internet request, each one lets the service see your device's IP address and basic technical details such as a user-agent string, which it handles under its own privacy policy. Apart from your iCloud sync and your own dictation (when Apple processes it), none of these requests contain your notes, your images, your name or any device identifier — only the identifier or address you typed.

05 Permissions

The app asks for these permissions only for the features listed. You can change them at any time in Settings (iPhone and iPad) or System Settings (Mac), under Privacy & Security.

  • Microphone — to dictate a topic in Capture, and to record into Sticky Notes.
  • Speech Recognition — to turn your dictated topic into text in Capture.
  • Camera (iPhone and iPad) — only to scan the ISBN barcode on a book. The scanner reads barcodes only.
  • Screen & System Audio Recording (Mac) — only if you choose to record System Audio. Only the audio is used.
  • Background audio (iPhone and iPad) — lets a recording continue while the phone is locked.
  • Photos and files — you choose images with the system photo picker or file picker, so the app receives only the items you pick and doesn't ask for access to your whole library. On the Mac, the app can reach only the files and folders you choose: images you import or save, lecture decks, PDFs you save and your transcript folder.

The app doesn't ask for your location, contacts or calendars, and it doesn't ask to track you.

06 Third parties

  • Apple provides iCloud sync and, in the case described above, speech recognition.
  • Crossref, doi.org, NCBI PubMed, Google Books, Open Library and Hugging Face receive only the requests listed in section 4, and only when those features are used.
  • WhisperKit, the open-source package by Argmax that runs the speech model, is the app's only third-party code package. It runs inside the app. In the version the app ships with, it contains no analytics and contacts only Hugging Face, to download the speech model and its tokenizer.
  • There are no analytics, crash-reporting, advertising or tracking services in the app.
  • We don't sell, rent or share your data, because we don't have it.

07 Children

Medical Casebook is meant for medical professionals and trainees and is not directed at children. It collects no data from anyone, including children.

08 Your choices and deleting your data

  • Use it without iCloud. Sync is optional. To keep your casebook out of iCloud sync, turn off iCloud for Casebook in your device's iCloud settings, or sign out of iCloud. Your casebook then stays on that device, and in any device backups you make, such as iCloud Backup, a Finder backup or Time Machine.
  • Trash. Deleted topics (and everything in them), Sticky Notes and Follow Ups stay in Trash for 30 days and are then deleted for good. You can Restore them, or remove them sooner with Delete Now or Empty Trash. Images don't go to Trash. On iPhone and iPad, deleting an image asks you to confirm; on the Mac, Delete Image acts immediately. In both cases it can't be undone.
  • The speech model. On the Mac, turn off Record in Sticky Notes in Settings › Recording; the app asks you to confirm, then removes the model. On iPhone and iPad, choose Remove from This Device in More › Recording. While Recording is off, the app won't download the model again.
  • Transcript files you saved to a folder on the Mac are ordinary files; delete them in Finder.
  • Deleting the app. On iPhone and iPad, deleting the app removes its data from that device, including the speech model and any recovery files. On the Mac, deleting the app leaves its data behind. To remove it, also delete the folder ~/Library/Containers/com.radiology.Radiology-Casebook, along with any transcript files or PDFs you saved elsewhere.
  • Your iCloud copy stays in your iCloud account until you delete it. You can do that in your device's iCloud settings, under the option to manage your iCloud storage.

Because we hold no data about you, there is nothing for us to export or delete on our side — and we can't recover your data for you.

09 Medical information

Medical Casebook is a personal study tool, not a medical record. It's designed for what you learn from articles, textbooks and teaching. It isn't intended for patient care.

  • Please don't enter information that could identify a patient — names, dates of birth, medical record or accession numbers, addresses, faces, or images with identifying text burned in. Remove identifiers from an image before you import it.
  • Images are stored as you import them, including any metadata embedded in the file, such as when and where a photo was taken. Remove identifying metadata as well as visible identifiers before you import.
  • Record only where you're allowed to. Case conferences often mention patients: don't record sessions where patients are identified, and remove any identifiers that end up in a transcript.
  • The app isn't designed for HIPAA compliance, and the developer doesn't enter into business associate agreements. Your institution's policies may also apply to what you store.
  • Content you sync is stored in your iCloud account under Apple's iCloud terms.
