    Recording

    Verbatim, on your device

    Press Record in a sticky note and English speech is transcribed by Whisper, running on-device through WhisperKit. You get a verbatim transcript, not an AI summary. It's automatic, so it can mishear: check important details against the source.

    • —Audio is held in memory and never written to disk.
    • —Many words Whisper was unsure of appear as [word?], and stretches it couldn't make out as [inaudible]. Not every error is marked.
    • —Each block is labeled “Transcribed from a voice recording. May contain errors.”
    • —The speech model (about 630 MB) downloads when you turn Recording on, and again only if it's removed or goes missing.
    • —Record only where you're allowed to. Case conferences often mention patients: don't record sessions where patients are identified, and remove any identifiers that end up in a transcript.

    On the Mac

    Lectures, calls and videos

    Record from the microphone, or from System Audio for a Zoom or Teams lecture or a video.

    • —System Audio uses macOS's Screen & System Audio Recording permission. Only the audio is kept; nothing on your screen is saved.
    • —If you record a call or meeting, make sure the other participants agree.
    • —Optionally, Save transcripts to a folder: one .txt file per recording, and the note is left as it was.
    • —A chip in the title bar shows a running recording from any section.

    On iPhone & iPad

    In your pocket, in the room

    When you stop, the transcript is added to the bottom of the note. If you leave the note while recording, a Recording · Stop bar sits above the tab bar.

    • —Recording continues with the phone locked.
    • —Uses the built-in microphone, even with AirPods connected.
    • —An interrupted recording is delivered the next time you open the app.
    • —Needs an iPhone with an A15 chip or newer (iPhone 13 or later, or iPhone SE 3rd generation), or an iPad with an A15, A16, A17 Pro or M-series chip.

    Study · Mac, iPhone, iPad

    Your notes, on a schedule

    Every phrase you hid in an Answered topic becomes a cloze card, built from its own sentence. Turn on Include images and your Repository joins the deck.

    • 01Phrase cards from Hide for study, with siblings from the same paragraph held back so one card doesn't give away the next.
    • 02Image cards show a starred picture bare; the answer is its title, caption and annotations.
    • 03Collection cards turn a starred collection into one card you page through.
    • 04A full-size viewer: pinch or ⌘+ to zoom, drag to pan, double-click to fit, arrow keys to page.
    • 05Grade Again, Hard, Good or Easy (keys 1–4). Set new cards a day, Practice anyway, Undo, and Save and Exit to resume later.

    Try it: Show answer, then grade the card.

    Study

    Images, collections & PDF

    The pictures that make the point

    Images live with their topic. Group them into collections with a modality and a confidence, split a collection into panels, and print the whole topic as a journal-style PDF.

    • —Annotations — arrow, arrowhead, asterisk or letter, in seven colors, each of which can be labeled with the structure it points to. The original image is never changed, and marks can be shown or hidden. Edited on the Mac, shown everywhere.
    • —Details per image — a caption title and caption, a label badge such as “Fig.: 12”, a confidence (Confirmed, Suspected, Unsure) and a link to where it came from.
    • —Collections carry a Modality (CT, MR, XR, US, NM, DSA, Other), hold panels, and can be reordered: drag on the Mac, or Move Up and Move Down.
    • —Full-screen viewer with a filmstrip; arrow keys step through a panel, ⌥-arrows jump between panels. On the Mac, Compare puts two images side by side with linked zoom.
    • —The Repository gathers everything you starred into one grid, filtered by Specialty, Anatomy, Disease, Kind and Modality. Removing something from it never deletes it.
    • —Typeset — running header, title, tags, a two-column body with a drop cap, figures in a framed two-up grid with annotations drawn on and listed, and references.
    • —Add to Section — on the Mac, right-click an image and choose Add to Section to print it right after the section it belongs to, instead of with the figures at the end. PDFs exported on any device keep the placement.
    • —Fill in the Blank — hidden phrases become numbered blanks, with an answer key, or without one for handing out.
    • —Save or share the PDF from Mac, iPhone or iPad.

    Follow Ups & Dashboard · Mac, iPhone, iPad

    Come back to it, and see it add up

    A follow up is a promise to come back to something on a given day, with an optional note and one attachment: an image, a collection or a topic. The Dashboard keeps score.

    • —Follow Ups sort into Overdue, Today, Later and Completed. Tick one off, reschedule it, edit it, or add a new one and attach an image, collection or topic. (There are no reminder notifications.)
    • —The Dashboard shows your Streak, Today's Goal (topics newly marked Answered), By Specialty, Activity, and Flashcards: accuracy over time, weakest areas, upcoming reviews and card progress. Reorder its cards in Dashboard Settings.

    Private by design

    We run no servers, so there's nothing for us to leak

    Medical Casebook has no accounts, no analytics and no developer servers. Your casebook lives on your devices and syncs through your own private iCloud database, which the developer cannot access. Recordings are transcribed on your device, and the audio is never saved.

    The app goes online only for iCloud sync, the source lookups you ask for, downloading the speech model if you turn on Recording, and Apple's speech recognition when you dictate a topic in Capture on a device that can't recognize speech on its own.

    Requirements

    What you need

    Mac
    macOS 26.3 or later. A native Mac app.
    iPhone & iPad
    iOS or iPadOS 26.4 or later.
    App version
    Version 2.9 or later for everything on this page.
    Sync
    Optional. Sign in to iCloud to sync between devices. No app account.
    Recording
    Transcribes English speech. On iPhone and iPad: an iPhone with an A15 chip or newer (iPhone 13 or later, or iPhone SE 3rd generation), or an iPad with an A15, A16, A17 Pro or M-series chip; older devices aren't supported. On the Mac, Apple silicon is recommended.
    Speech model
    About 630 MB, downloaded when you turn Recording on. On iPhone and iPad you need about 1 GB free (the download plus room for the system to prepare the model), and Wi-Fi is recommended. Remove it any time to free the space.
    Network
    Only for iCloud sync, source lookups you ask for, the model download, and Apple's speech recognition for Capture dictation when your device can't do it on its own. Your casebook itself is stored on your device.
